Photography



As with writing, I felt the instinct to photograph long before I was really equipped to be able to do so. I remember the familiar itch I felt as a child when a curved bittersweet branch or a soft patch of melting snow caught my eye; I wanted desperately to freeze the image and weave it with the feeling in my chest, to capture the scene and make what it meant to me legible to others. Satisfying the impulse at the time usually meant taking a picture with my pocket-sized digital camera or writing a paragraph describing what I saw. In college, I started printing the images I took and writing long, meandering captions, hoarding away the moments in my journal.


I’m not the first to have noticed or embody the relationship between writing and photography. Many of my favorite artists are both writers and photographers; often both at once (Sophie Calle, Patti Smith). I noticed that there were more English majors than art majors in the photography class where I learned darkroom photography. What is it, exactly, that makes writing and photographing two forms of the same impulse? They both document, tell stories, and assert the importance of a subject, yes, but I think there’s more to explore, especially when paired together. Susan Sontag writes, “[photographs] are a grammar and, even more importantly, an ethics of seeing.” …“They document, interpret, and perhaps most essentially, define the photographers position to its subject” (“In Plato’s Cave,” in On Photography, 1977). They are ideoloigy replicated/reinforced; identity forcing itself as an organizing force upon the world A way of understanding my ethics, of defining who I am through the pictures I take. Its all about the ego after all !!


Most of the images here I processed and printed myself in the Smith College darkrooms. Having lost consistent access to a darkroom post-college, I have trouble photographing at all; to ship off my precious film to be developed by another’s hands, or worse—by a machine—is like .. . Not to mention its an utter money pit. So yes, some of these were also shot on my iphone. Do you think the convenience of the iphone undermines the art of the photograph?
